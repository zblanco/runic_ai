# *The Wandering Inn* — Chapter 10.59 T Wiki Page

## Overview
This chapter is a dense Tenbault-focused crossover of several threads: **Tessa / Shriekblade**’s deteriorating condition and unstable identity, **Merdon**’s role as handler and investigator, the city’s rotten **healing economy under Hekusha**, and a broader convergence of outside forces including **Magnolia Reinhart**, **Teriarch**, **Lyonette**, and **Gadrea**.

The main tension is between **healing and damage**. Tenbault presents itself as a miracle city, but that miracle is rationed, monetized, and entangled with corruption, lotteries, forged names, and desperation. Meanwhile, Tessa is physically and mentally collapsing under repeated drug use and unreliable memories, yet remains valuable enough to be repeatedly used as a tool in crisis response. In parallel, Magnolia and Teriarch’s carriage thread shows another kind of broken system: leadership, regret, and old mistakes being turned back into action.

A major late-thread development is **Gadrea** entering Tenbault and reacting to the city’s healing/faith environment as something real but deeply wrong. This reframes the city’s problems not just as civic decay, but as moral and spiritual corruption.

**Provenance note:** merged from the provided notes for slices 1–13. Tessa’s memory-heavy material is explicitly unreliable in several places.

## Events by Actor

### Tessa / Shriekblade
- Appears in severe physical decline: thin, bloodshot-eyed, coughing phlegm and blood, bleeding from nose and mouth, and possibly risking blindness from drug abuse.
- Has torn-off scales in a checkerboard pattern, likely to allow repeated needle access and injections.
- Frames **Hekusha** as an “escape plan,” imagining restoration as a way to get clean later, though this reads as self-deception.
- Uses **King’s Draught** and other substances despite knowing the consequences; the drugs quickly alter her perception and push her into euphoric, reckless behavior.
- Later in the chapter, appears in a hallucination-heavy breakfast scene where she misreads context and rank before drifting into unstable recollection of “Captain Groms.”
- Injures **Merge** during a distorted combat episode after perceiving violence incorrectly.
- In the serial-killer investigation thread, vomits after food and is identified by Merdon as likely being on **Roama’s Fancy**, possibly mixed with King’s Draught.
- Admits or half-recognizes the hallucination of “Captain Groms,” showing she cannot cleanly separate memory, drug effect, and present reality.
- Recounts a traumatic and compromised Winter Solstice fragment involving **Draugr**, Roshal assassins, a failed job, a kidnapped client, and stabbing allies.
- Later helps in a murder investigation in the Plague Quarter, identifying the victim’s wound as a clean blade strike and ruling out ordinary [Assassins].
- Repeatedly functions as a dangerous but necessary asset: unstable, high-value, and hard to trust.

### Crowdcaller Merdon / Groms
- Observes Tenbault with the sense that it is static, sealed, and fortress-like despite wider world change.
- Responds to Tessa with resignation and familiarity rather than panic, implying he has seen her cycle through this pattern before.
- Acts as handler, witness, and reluctant enforcer around Tessa’s drug abuse and violence.
- In the breakfast/hallucination sequence, tries to keep the situation contained and prevent further escalation.
- During the serial-killer investigation, coordinates the city response and recognizes that the case involves **two killers in two places**, not one.
- Uses sound-based abilities such as **[Cone of Sound]**, **[Stunning Shout]**, and **[Voice: Remove Recoil]**.
- Examines the Plague Quarter corpse and concludes the killing was done with sharp blades rather than a standard [Assassin] method.
- Helps manage the moral and logistical problem of Tessa’s instability while still using her investigative instincts.

### Hekusha / the Healer of Tenbault
- Remains central to Tessa’s fantasy of “reset” and recovery.
- Is discussed as having once been a genuinely good healer before becoming warped by money, power, resentment, and responsibility.
- Oversees Tenbault’s healing system, including clerks, lottery access, and the Plague Quarter.
- Uses multiple healing-related Skills, including **[Normative Healing]**, **[Reduced Mana Cost: Healing]**, **[Idealized Restoration]**, and **[Restoration]**.
- Faces falling business, counterfeit money, counterfeit scrolls, and competition from the **Last Light**.
- Orders watchers to monitor the lotteries for forged names.
- Uses **[Healer’s Orders]** coercively to push Tessa into compliance and a dangerous assignment.
- Treats Tessa as both liability and asset, refusing to simply discard her.
- Later hosts Ailendamus visitors and negotiates over healing **Gadrea**, exposing how Tenbault’s power structure handles rare cases.

### Teriarch
- Repairs Magnolia’s damaged carriage, replacing degraded materials with enchanted ones and rebuilding the spell structure.
- Works with materials like **ironwood**, **Haelbrass fittings**, and **Salazsarian Diamond** windows.
- Describes or installs carriage enchantments such as **[Soundless Wheels]**, **[Sustained Invisibility]**, and **[Scythes of Chariel]**.
- Is embarrassed by Magnolia’s practical control over the carriage’s appearance, especially when she orders it painted cherry blossom pink.
- Uses the repair process to push Magnolia toward taking action and making larger impact.
- Is physically training himself again and preparing to depart toward the New Lands.
- Suffers motion sickness and bodily abuse when Magnolia uses the carriage’s hidden combat function.

### Magnolia Reinhart
- Takes personal responsibility for past failures, especially in connection with Tenbault, though others frame the blame as broader and more complicated.
- Insists on practicality over ornamentation, then surprises everyone by turning the carriage pink.
- Reasserts control over household and travel logistics, including drivers, seating, and backup plans.
- Reflects on old political violence and resents that younger people are cleaning up her mess.
- Reveals or uses **“Trebuchet Mode”**, a combat function that flings the carriage at bandits.
- Demonstrates that she is more active and dangerous than many assume.
- Challenges Teriarch with a wager involving impact on Izril, the **Crown of Flowers**, and possibly a **<Mythical Quest>**.
- Implies she is willing to use power directly rather than only as a symbol.

### Ressa / Ressga

- Advises on House Reinhart safety and household survival.
- Travels with Magnolia, armed and fully integrated into the operation.
- Reacts strongly to Magnolia’s bolder choices and the carriage stunts.

### Lilian Woods / Gadrea / Knives / Lady Eliasor

### Gadrea
- Refuses to settle immediately with Lilian, Knives, or Lady Eliasor, saying she must go south to understand something before she dies.
- Arrives in Tenbault and gives away jewelry and money to the poor, telling them to leave and survive.
- Cleans stables herself and negotiates lodging by labor rather than status or wealth.
- Senses the city’s faith toward the healer as intense, cloying, and wrong.
- Gets swept into the Plague Quarter during a disease-control action and improvises protections such as facemasks.
- Investigates the spiritual pattern of the city’s faith and believes it could change everything, though Tenbault’s version feels corrupted by desperation.
- Becomes a key contrast to the city: generous, physically powerful, and searching for meaning, but not naïve.

### Adventurer team / Hekusha’s headquarters / Merdon’s unit

- The **Last Light** is mentioned as a controversial healer figure.
- Merdon manages a hungover adventurer team, uses sound-force to silence one of them, and threatens serial-killer duty if they fail lottery oversight.

## Plot Points
- Tessa’s condition is physically catastrophic: blood loss, phlegm, needle damage, possible blindness, and repeated drug use.
- Hekusha is not a clean cure; Tessa treats her as an escape mechanism while Merdon knows the cycle is recurring.
- Tenbault’s healing economy is heavily rationed and socially controlled through lotteries, clerks, and money.
- The city has an active serial killer problem, and the investigation expands from a single killer to two separate killers in two places.
- Lottery forgery is a concrete corruption issue tied to healing access.
- Magnolia and Teriarch’s carriage becomes both a transport system and a weapon platform.
- **Trebuchet Mode** is a real combat function.
- Lyonette’s group moves north because she refuses to leave people behind.
- Gadrea’s arrival links the healing thread to a broader faith/spiritual phenomenon in Tenbault.
- Shriekblade remains useful despite instability, especially in detective and crisis roles.

## Reveals
- Tessa’s memories are unreliable, and some flashback material is explicitly compromised.
- Tessa is likely using multiple drugs, especially **King’s Draught** and **Roama’s Fancy**.
- Hekusha was once genuinely good before power and resentment altered her.
- Hekusha’s healing is unusually strong and can sometimes restore beyond normal limits, though not consistently.
- Merdon knows Tessa extremely well and has likely seen many self-destructive cycles.
- Tessa rejects the identity **Shriekblade** with “That’s not my name,” implying contested identity or dissociation.
- Tenbault’s healing system depends on scarcity and controlled access rather than universal care.
- Gadrea is far older and more dangerous than she first appears.
- Faith in Tenbault is not merely symbolic; Gadrea can sense it as a tangible force.
- The Plague Quarter is a maintained civic containment zone, not just a neglected district.
- Merdon’s sound abilities are substantial, including force projection and shock effects.
- Tessa’s role in the city is double-edged: disaster and indispensable specialist.

## Takeaways
- The chapter’s emotional center is **repetition under damage**: Tessa cycles through drugs, collapse, and usefulness; Hekusha’s city cycles through rationing and desperation; Merdon keeps the whole system barely functioning.
- **Tenbault is a miracle city built on misery.** Healing exists, but it is expensive, controlled, and morally compromised.
- Magnolia and Teriarch offer a parallel thread of regret becoming action: repair, leadership, and a willingness to use power directly.
- Gadrea introduces a spiritual counterpoint, moving through the same broken city with generosity and a sense that something deeper is wrong.
- The chapter keeps asking whether people can be restored without being broken into something else first.
